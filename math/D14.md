## Finite-person expected-matching update

[P] `adoption_population_declaration.json` freezes N fixed actors, binary
membership, uniform random focal actor and uniformly matched partner among
the other N−1 actors. A participating actor earns a if its partner participates,
pays recurring c and receives founder subsidys. Nonparticipation gives0.
A new participant additionally pays one-time entry feee; re-entry pays again.
The focal actor compares expected **one-date** counterfactual payoffs before
matching and takes a strict improvement; ties retain membership. This is a
declared myopic update, not a forward-looking equilibrium/empirical learning
law. Protected care is separate and unaffected by any participation decision,
subsidy expiry or license loss.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** At population countk,

    L(k)=a(k−1)/(N−1)−c+s   (incumbent keep−exit),
    J(k)=a k/(N−1)−c+s−e   (outsider join−stay),
    u_k=(N−k)/N * 1{J(k)>0},
    d_k=k/N * 1{L(k)<0},
    P(k,k+1)=u_k, P(k,k−1)=d_k, P(k,k)=1−u_k−d_k.

[B: lab] The matching denominator excludes self. A hypothetical entrant sees
k existing partners, not k−1; it pays entry only on switching. Symmetry makes
the count chain an exact lumping of binary individual membership configurations.
Counts where u=d=0 are exactly the one-date no-profitable-switch membership
profiles under these state-dependent entry rules. This is not a certificate
for an intertemporal game with expected future subsidies or learning.

## Potential, finite absorption and tipping classification

[P] N≥2,a>0,e≥0; c,s are fixed real stage quantities. Mutation/tremble is0
for this section, and ties retain membership.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** The finite count potential

    Φ_s(k)=a k(k−1)/[2(N−1)]−(c−s)k

[B: lab] rises on every actual strict switch: upward difference is J(k)+e>0,
downward difference is−L(k)>0. Therefore no count repeats after a switch;
there are at most N switches before absorption. In a nonabsorbing count at
least one actor can switch, so probability of a switch is≥1/N each fresh
update. Geometric waiting gives almost-sure finite absorption and expected
updates≤N². This establishes that the exact transient inverse used below is
valid, rather than assuming all non-endpoint states are transient.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** Both directional rates are positive iff

    (N−1)(c−s+e)/a < k < 1+(N−1)(c−s)/a.

[B: lab] Its width is1−(N−1)e/a. If e<a/(N−1), there is at most one integer
critical countj; below it is downward-only, above upward-only, assuming0,N
are absorbing. Atj every focal actor switches, so the probability of moving
up is(N−j)/N and downj/N. Subsequent paths cannot return across the critical
count. Hence eventual full-adoption probability is0 belowj,1 abovej and
(N−j)/N atj. If there is no critical integer the tipping step is deterministic.
If e≥a/(N−1), no count is bidirectional, and an interval with J≤0≤L can
hold stable partial populations. Entry friction therefore changes the type
of tipping object, not merely a continuous mean-field fraction.

## Founder support and control are separate

[P] In an independent continuing protocol, founder departure removes subsidy
only and retains matching benefit a2. In a founder-exclusive license control,
departure also makes operational matching benefit0. Ownership/access is an
actual premise, not inferred from the label “founder”. Care remains protected
independently in both models.

[B: lab] **Grade: computed exact.** A fully adopted population remains at6 in
the continuing zero-subsidy protocol. Under license revocation every incumbent
strictly leaves, no outsider joins, and0 is the only absorber. Starting from6
expected collapse time is6(1+1/2+...+1/6)=147/10 updates. An institution can
therefore survive the loss of financing while failing the loss of operational
control; neither is established by an adoption curve alone.

## Positive noise removes permanent adoption

[P] A choice trembleζ1/100 independently flips the selected actor's preferred
binary choice; type hazardρ and repair rateγ remain different variables.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** Then
u_k=(N−k)/N[ζ+(1−2ζ)1{J>0}] and
d_k=k/N[ζ+(1−2ζ)1{L<0}]. Every adjacent pair communicates; with staying rows
the finite chain has a positive unique stationary law. Birth-death detailed
balance computes weights w0=1,w_k=w_{k−1}u_{k−1}/d_k and normalizes them.
Full adoption is no longer absorbing: the probability of staying there forever
is lim(1−ζ)^t=0. Indeed each state can hit0 within N steps with probability
at least(ζ/N)^N, so failure to hit0 for m blocks is at most
[1−(ζ/N)^N]^m→0. Thus eventual loss of full adoption is almost sure even if
its stationary occupancy is high. This is turnover, not a permanence theorem.
