# C9 — constitutional succession versus laundering

[P] State includes version, creditor debt and retained receipt. Old/new debt
can be0 or1. Issuer0 is authorized by a separately fixed registry; issuer1 is
not. A lawful version update must preserve the old debt. A current panel
observes only the ordinary service
acknowledgement0; it does not read the registry, prior debt or revision receipt.
Authenticity and retained delivery of those records are explicit premises.

[B: lab] **Grade: computed exact.** A version change by issuer0 retaining debt1
and a laundering attempt by issuer1 setting debt0 have equal panel outputs0.
The receipt audit recovers issuer and old/new debt, returning lawful/failed
respectively. `python3 covenant_lab/carriers.py` checks those exact comparisons
and all eight receipt combinations. Redacting issuer identifies(0,1,1) with
(1,1,1); redacting old debt identifies(0,1,1) with(0,0,1). Each pair has one
lawful and one failed record, proving the corresponding warrant loss.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** No function of the panel output alone
can recover lawfulness on these two records; it is not fiber-constant. The
expanded receipt carrier does preserve that target, by explicit readout. A
lawful change revises version with the retained obligations; a current-content
match cannot make an unauthorized debt deletion equivalent to it.

[B] This gives a finite provenance/readout construction, not cryptographic
unforgeability, actual constitutional legitimacy or an autonomous public audit.
