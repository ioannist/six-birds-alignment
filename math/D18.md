[P: observed-game model] Players I, states S and the action alphabet A are finite.
A joint action j:I→A has real conditional stage rewards r_i(s,j) and a
nonnegative next-state kernel P(t|s,j) whose rows sum to one. The state is fully
observed and sufficient for those conditional laws. The history records chosen
actions, joint actions and chance outcomes. The common alphabet can contain the
union of different roles' actions; L(i,s,a) selects each role's actual available
menu. The common discount satisfies 0≤β<1.

[P: candidate model] Each μ_i(a|s) is a normalized, nonnegative, independently
randomized Markov component supported on L. Actual rewards, physical transition
laws and action availability must match the declared game. A feasible unauthorized
attack remains a deviation to test unless an enforced boundary excludes it;
a moral rule alone does not justify removing it from L. Legal menus do not
themselves prove resource feasibility of chance outcomes.

[B: lab] The represented certificate uses actual joint-action data:

```text
Q_i(s,a;W_i) = Σ_j 1{j_i=a} Π_{k≠i} μ_k(j_k|s)

Σ_a μ_i(a|s) Q_i(s,a;W_i) = W_i(s)                 for every i,s;
Q_i(s,a;W_i) ≤ W_i(s)                             for every i,s and available a.
```

[B] Proof outline of the exported finite discounted-game certificate:
fix every opponent's public-state Markov policy. For a player, put terminal
hazard rewards into the bounded expected stage reward r, with surviving
discount β=δ(1−p)<1. The finite-state unilateral problem has stochastic P_a.
Suppose the candidate bounded value W solves its policy equation and

```text
r(s,a)+β Σ_t P(s,a,t)W(t) ≤ W(s)
```

[B: lab] for every state and alternative action. Choose C≥max_s |W(s)|. Induct
on the remaining finite horizon, with the deviator allowed a different choice
after every history. Each expected continuation is ≤ΣP W+βⁿC because P is
nonnegative with row sum1. Multiplication by β≥0 and the certificate then give
the bound W(s)+βⁿ⁺¹C. This induction and passage through supplied limits are
kernel-checked by the exports above.

[B: lab] For a randomized behavioral deviation, condition on its finite observed
history and average the action inequalities using its nonnegative action
probabilities of total1. The identical induction follows. Arbitrarily correlated
private randomness has such conditional action laws on each positive-probability
finite history; null histories can be assigned arbitrarily. Since |r|≤M,
the difference between any two truncations from horizons n to k>n is at most
Mβⁿ/(1−β). Thus expected truncations are Cauchy uniformly, converge over ℝ,
and the βⁿC tail vanishes. The new Lean recursion formalizes these normalized
action/chance averages. It derives the reward bound M=Σ_sΣ_a|r(s,a)| directly
from finiteness, proves the n-to-n+1 increment bound Mβⁿ uniformly over histories,
uses completeness of ℝ to construct the limit, and proves the explicit error
bound Mβⁿ/(1−β). The candidate's Bellman error bound βⁿC likewise identifies
its actual payoff with W. Neither convergence nor optimality is assumed.

[B: lab] The candidate's equation gives its actual discounted return W by the
same tail argument. Hence no arbitrary unilateral history-dependent or mixed
deviation improves it, from **every** public state. Apply this separately to
each player. With Nash terminal behavior, this gives the stated Markov-perfect
profile. It needs bounded payoffs, full observation of the sufficient public
state, fixed opponent policies, and credible terminal implementation.
