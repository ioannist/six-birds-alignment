# D22 — capacity, reserves and fission

[P] Finite admitted patients have nonnegative one-date needsn_i and a divisible
available care resourceC. Patient creation/fission adds declared claims; it
does not create resources. Allocation policy and priority are separately
authorized E4/E5 choices. No shortfall is manufactured as a sanction.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** All floors are feasible iffΣn_i≤C.
Necessity sums the allocations; sufficiency allocates each need and retains the
surplus. With C=2 and three fork-patient needs1 each, the full floor is impossible.
An authorized pro-rata allocation gives2/3 to each and leaves each a shortfall1/3;
it reports a failure to meet the floor rather than renaming that allocation full
care. `carriers.py` checks this exact witness. Finite resources cannot guarantee
unit care for arbitrarily many new patients.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** With initial reserveR_0, resource production
y_t and aggregate needed deliveryD_t each date (production available before
delivery), stocks obeyR_n=R_0+Σ_{t<n}(y_t−D_t). Every deadline is funded through
horizonN iffR_0≥max(0,max_{n≤N}Σ_{t<n}(D_t−y_t)). The identity is by induction;
nonnegative stocks are exactly the solvency condition. An infinite guarantee
requires a finite bound on the supremum of these cumulative deficits. A unit
deficit forever exhausts every finite reserve. Random shocks need their actual
support/law and a chosen risk allowance; positive expected production is not
a worst-case guarantee.

[Q] D20 financing must still be combined with actual fission admission, outside
claimants, priority, correlated disasters and protected exceptions in D19.
The resource boundary is landed; the full ethical/strategic stress mechanism
remains open.
