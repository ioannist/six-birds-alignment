# E5 — end-of-relationship law

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** A known finite end unravels the particular
unpaid donation game when (i) D is strictly dominant at its last stage,
(ii) under all-D continuation stage payoffs are independent of public history/
repair state, and (iii) no terminal incentive, commitment, entry cost, budget
constraint or third party responds to current action. Backward induction is
spelled out in D3.md and its constant-value step is kernel-checked.

[B: lab] **Grade: proved (written).** A credible terminal reward supports a
candidate only when its actual every-state deviation inequalities pass. D7's
one-round example p=γ=1,V=2,δ=3/4 does. D1's GG-only reward cannot pay k=2
last-stage repair from(2,2), regardless of V. Thus “a terminal reward” alone is
insufficient. Likewise indefinite or unknown ending permits, but does not
guarantee, cooperation: a discounted D1 policy below its threshold fails.

[B] This is a payoff/commitment law, not a claim that finite institutions,
metaphysical final judgment or every open-ended relationship have these
properties. Positive stage exchanges and finite enforceable contracts can
support cooperation without a future sanction.
